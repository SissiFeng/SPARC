Sparse Predictive Aggregation of Regression Components

Let the evidence decide which models get a say.

In a self-driving laboratory, the model that picks the next experiment is usually chosen before the first one is run. SPARC keeps that choice open. Every new measurement can change which models influence the next decision, and which existing measurements deserve a second look.

The algorithm in six moves

Each figure below runs the real computation in your browser on a small one-dimensional example: 20 noisy measurements of a hidden response curve, and one model from each of SPARC's four structural families. Press play, drag the sliders, hover the plots.

MOVE 01

Sparse data leave several models standing

Twenty measurements cover the left side of the design space. A straight line, a tree, a smooth kernel model and a monotonic fit all describe them reasonably well. Where nobody has measured yet, they tell four different stories.

Committing to one of them now would decide where the campaign goes next. SPARC keeps all four in play until the data can tell them apart.

The full method uses a library of 12 models grouped into these four families.

MOVE 02

Score each model on data it has not seen

SPARC hides one measurement, refits every model on the rest, and asks each model to predict the hidden value. Doing this for every measurement fills the evidence matrix: one row per measurement, one column per model.

Every entry is a genuine out-of-sample prediction. A model earns influence by predicting what it was not shown, never by how closely it fits its own training data.

P[i, m] = fm(−i)(xi)
MOVE 03

Keep the models that keep getting picked

A model that looks useful on one draw of the data may not on the next. SPARC redraws 80% of the records 50 times, rebuilds the evidence each time, and fits a sparse non-negative regression of the measurements on the models' predictions.

Models selected in at least 60% of the draws enter the active set. The others receive a weight of exactly zero.

In the paper, screening applies once 20 or more records are available.

MOVE 04

Weigh the survivors, and keep the uncertainty

The surviving models share influence through non-negative weights that sum to one. SPARC does not settle on a single set of weights. It samples many plausible sets from a posterior with sparse hierarchical priors, so a model the data cannot yet rule out keeps a share.

Each dot is one draw. A wide cloud means the data still allow very different ways of combining the models.

wm = |am| / Σj|aj|,  y ~ N(Pw, σ²)
MOVE 05

Where the models agree, trust is high

Push every weight draw through the fitted models and you get a bundle of composed predictions. The bundle is tight where the retained models agree and fans out where they diverge, even though every candidate shares the same posterior over weights.

SPARC combines this candidate-specific spread with a residual scale into a trust score between 0 and 1.

T(x) = 1 / (1 + √(u(x)² + ures²) / q)

Trust is a decision score on the response scale. It is not a calibrated probability that a prediction is correct.

Hover the plot to read a candidate.
MOVE 06

Trust tempers exploration

The next experiment maximises predicted response plus an exploration bonus. The bonus grows where the models disagree, and trust scales it back as the search moves into poorly supported regions, so the bonus can never exceed β·q.

Then the experiment is run, the result joins the record, and all six moves repeat with one more row of evidence.

a(x) = μ(x) + β · u(x) · T(x)

Run the loop and watch the weights move

Each click runs the recommended experiment on the hidden response curve, adds the result to the record and repeats all six moves. The first measurement lands where the models disagree most, and one model pays for its confident guess.

    Then question the record itself

    A closed loop inherits every faulty measurement in its record. SPARC screens existing records with four independent diagnostics and prioritizes those with two or more flags for repetition, before anyone knows whether they will repeat.

    Nonconformity. How unusual is this record's held-out residual compared with the others?

    Influence. How far does a simple reference fit move when this record is left out?

    Isolation. How easily does an isolation forest separate this record from the rest?

    Mahalanobis distance. How far is it from the centre of the feature space?

    A flag asks for more evidence. It does not show that a measurement is wrong. Try it: slip a gross error into the record, see which lamps light, then repeat the flagged records and their nearest unflagged neighbours.

    What the paper reports

    Seven results, replayed from the numbers in the manuscript. Each animation ends on the reported values.

    RESULT 01

    Eleven predictions, fixed before measurement

    SPARC was fitted to 125 initial experiments and recommended 11 new conditions for an automated alkaline oxygen-evolution campaign. Each hollow marker is a prediction recorded before the experiment. Each filled marker is what the platform then measured.

    Overpotential at 10 mA cm⁻². Eight of eleven fell below 300 mV, with a best value of 242 mV, against 18 of 125 in the initial record. Mean absolute prediction error 26.7 mV. Individual values are read from Supplementary Fig. 6. This compares two sets of experiments; it is not a randomized trial.

    RESULT 02

    Flagged before anyone repeated them

    Seven records carrying two or more diagnostic flags were repeated, together with eight unflagged controls matched in feature space. Group assignments were fixed before the repeat measurements.

    Absolute difference between original and repeated overpotential. All three deviations above 130 mV are flagged records, and every control is below 42 mV. Means 76 and 27 mV; medians 36.3 and 32.5 mV. Individual values are read from Fig. 5f. Descriptive summary of small groups.

    RESULT 03

    Eight published datasets, no extra descriptors

    The benchmarks span molecular, polymeric and inorganic materials. SPARC used the descriptors already available for each task and was evaluated by nested leave-one-out.

    Predictive R². Hollow marker: value reported by the source study. Filled marker: SPARC. SPARC is higher on six of eight, with a relative gain of up to 77% on OER stability. Source studies used their own evaluation splits, so their values give context and are not matched-split baselines.

    RESULT 04

    Take a component out

    Choose a component to remove or replace, and see what happens to R² on each of the eight datasets.

    Relative change in R² (%) from full SPARC. Single-run point estimates; the size of each contribution depends on the task.

    RESULT 05

    More information was not always better

    Pretrained representations were appended to SPARC's descriptors on two small datasets. Separately, GPT-5.2 was allowed to choose among SPARC's existing feature strategies and model hypotheses.

    Leave-one-out R² on 160 records (OER stability) and 99 records (polymer HER). The vertical line marks SPARC with its own descriptors. All six pretrained representations lowered R². The language-model selector raised one task and lowered the other. Single point estimates.

    RESULT 06

    A search race in a finite pool

    Archived outcomes were hidden and revealed one at a time. All three strategies start from the same initial experiments, and each runner stops when its best value so far comes within 0.1% of the pool optimum.

    Evaluations, including the shared initialization (20 for Ru dissolution, 10 for polymer HER). One search from one initial set per task, so these are case studies and not estimates of average efficiency.

    RESULT 07

    Ranking candidates beyond the measured range

    Models trained on the initial region ranked candidates outside it. Each dot is one candidate in the first acquired batch, which covers a quarter of the extrapolation pool.

    Filled dots are in the true top quartile of the pool: 35% for formate oxidation and 43% for OER activity, against 25% expected at random. Predictive uncertainty was 1.27 and 1.75 times that of the initial region.
